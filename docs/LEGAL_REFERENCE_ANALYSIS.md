## 1. 23andMe

**Sources:** 23andme.com/legal/terms-of-service, 23andme.com/legal/privacy/full-version, 23andme.com/about/individual-data-consent

### Terms of Service

**Data Ownership:**
- Genetic information is provided to users, but: "Once you obtain your Genetic Information, the knowledge is irrevocable."
- "Product Development" is defined as "research performed for the purpose of new product development and new product development activities performed by 23andMe on De-identified Information."
- Research participation is "voluntary and based upon an Institutional Review Board-approved consent document."

**Revenue Sharing:**
- Users receive NO revenue share. The individual data consent explicitly states: "If research produces commercial products, these products or services will be the property of the researchers who developed them and you will not receive any compensation."
- 23andMe monetizes aggregate/de-identified data through licensing deals (e.g., GSK).

### Privacy Policy (Full Version)

**De-Identified Data:**
- De-identified information is defined as data "stripped of identifying data, such as name and contact information, so that an individual cannot reasonably be identified."
- Research analyses use information "stripped of your identifying Registration Information."
- Aggregate Information "does not contain information about, nor can it reasonably be linked to, a specific individual."

**Third-Party Sharing:**
- Shares with: service providers for sample processing/analysis, corporate affiliates, entities in mergers/acquisitions
- Does NOT share with: "public databases," "insurance companies or employers," or "law enforcement, absent a valid court order."

**Consent Mechanisms:**
- Research participation is strictly opt-in via "Main Research Consent"
- Allows use of "de-identified Genetic Information and/or Self-Reported Information...for research purposes"
- Individual data consent allows sharing of "de-identified individual-level data with approved researchers outside of 23andMe"

**Consent Revocation:**
- Users may withdraw consent anytime through account settings
- Company prevents further sharing within 30 days of withdrawal
- Critical limitation: "any Research involving your data that has already been performed or published prior to your withdrawal...will not be reversed"
- Previously shared data cannot be retrieved from research partners

### Individual Data Sharing Consent

**Scope:** Expands beyond main research consent to allow individual-level (not just pooled) data sharing with external researchers.

**De-Identification Process:** A code or "secure matching token" replaces personal identifiers. Removes name, date of birth, address.

**Research Partners:** Range from "academic institutions and non-profit organizations to pharmaceutical and diagnostic companies." Each partnership screened by 23andMe's scientific committee.

**Data Categories Shared:** Genetic information, self-reported details (age, ethnicity, survey responses), external medical records, platform interaction logs, microbiome data.

**Pharma Restrictions:** Partners must sign agreements restricting data use to approved projects and prohibiting re-identification attempts.

### GSK Data Licensing Agreement (SEC Filing)

**Financial Terms:**
- $20 million upfront payment for one-year, non-exclusive data license
- GSK invested $300 million in 23andMe as part of broader collaboration
- 23andMe eligible for downstream royalties under certain database uses
- Both parties retain royalties on active programs from initial collaboration
- 23andMe took royalty option on three programs GSK will independently advance

**Data Access Scope:**
- GSK receives de-identified, summary data from global genome- and phenome-wide analysis
- 12-month data access period
- 23andMe offers research services for data analyses

**Consent Basis:**
- Customer participation is voluntary, requires affirmative consent
- All information shared with GSK is de-identified; no individual identifiable to GSK

## 2. Nebula Genomics

**Sources:** nebula.org/blog/nebula-customer-user-terms-of-use, nebula.org/blog/privacy-policy

### Terms of Use

**Data Ownership:**
- Users retain ownership of uploaded genetic content
- Users grant Nebula "a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, transferable licence to use, adapt, reproduce, store and copy that content"
- Notably LACKS explicit statements about user ownership of raw genomic data generated from samples

**Revenue Sharing:**
- NO revenue-sharing mechanism mentioned
- No compensation for users whose data is sold or licensed
- No financial incentives for data contribution described

**Data Marketplace:**
- No data marketplace described in the Terms
- No user-to-user data trading or secondary market transactions
- No monetization pathways for users exist according to these Terms

**Third-Party Sharing:**
- Nebula reserves rights to share samples with third parties for "DNA extraction, sequencing or data analysis"
- Subject to Privacy Policy

**Consent:**
- "Participation in research is determined by your consent settings. You can change your consent settings in your Nebula user account under the Edit Profile section."

### Privacy Policy

**Genetic Data Definition:**
- "Genetic Data" = DNA from biological samples converted to machine-readable code, plus metadata (DNA test kit code, birth year, sex)
- Explicitly excludes de-identified data from this category

**De-Identified Data:**
- Permits unrestricted use: "We may use Personal Information...to create de-identified and aggregated data sets"
- Shares de-identified sets with third parties for research "in accordance with Part 46...of Title 45 of the Code of Federal Regulations"

**Data Sales:**
- "We will not provide any data (genetic or non-genetic) to an insurance company or employer"
- "We do not sell or share any Personal Information for a third party's cross-context behavioral advertising"

**Consent Mechanisms:**
- US users: "affirmative consent" required before research use of identifiable genetic data
- EU/UK users: "informed and explicit consent" for research involving personal information
- Research Pool participation does NOT automatically authorize specific research projects

**Consent Revocation:**
- Users can withdraw "at any time" via portal.nebula.org or emailing legal@nebula.org
- "Your decision to revoke consent will not impact your Services"

**Third-Party Sharing (with consent):**
- Partner laboratories (sample processing only)
- Approved research partners (academic, pharmaceutical, medical device companies)
- Law enforcement only when legally compelled: "We will not voluntarily share your Survey Data or Genetic Data with law enforcement"

## 3. Truveta

**Sources:** truveta.com/privacy-notice, truveta.com/legal/truveta-community-standard, truveta.com/members, truveta.com/truveta-genome-project/faq

*Note: Truveta's website uses dynamic content loading (Divi theme) that prevented full text extraction of legal documents. Information compiled from metadata, search results, and publicly available descriptions.*

### Business Model

- Collective of 30+ US health systems sharing de-identified patient data
- Dataset: 120+ million de-identified patients from 900+ hospitals and 20,000+ clinics
- Health systems are equity owners/members of Truveta
- Revenue model: Licenses de-identified data to researchers, pharma, and life sciences companies

### Data Use Terms

**Data Licensing:**
- "Truveta data is licensed for healthcare research, not for targeting advertising"
- Member health systems contribute de-identified EHR data
- Truveta enforces "compliant behavior of platform users in terms of taking proper precautions in using and sharing the data"

### Community Standard (Access Terms)

**Core Rules:**
- "Maintaining the privacy of data about patients is paramount"
- Users must not "upload any personally identifiable information to Truveta Studio"

### Privacy Notice

- Last modified: February 11, 2026
- Describes how personal information is "collected, used, shared, and protected, including cookies and privacy rights"
- Full text not extractable due to dynamic rendering

### Consent Model

- Truveta operates under HIPAA's de-identification framework
- Individual patient consent is NOT required because data is de-identified before leaving health systems
- Health systems handle HIPAA compliance for contributing data
- Patients cannot individually opt out of Truveta specifically (data is covered under health system HIPAA notices)

## 4. Datavant

**Sources:** datavant.com/privacy-policy, datavant.com white papers, datavant.com/products/datavant-connect

### Privacy Policy

**De-Identified Data:**
- "Datavant may create, use, or disclose de-identified information so long as the entities to whom Datavant discloses such de-identified data are prohibited from re-identifying or attempting to re-identify the information"

**Data Sales:**
- "We do not sell your Personal Information to third parties in exchange for monetary or other valuable consideration, nor do we share your Personal Information with third parties for cross-contextual behavioral advertising"
- California-specific: "We do not...Sell or share the personal information of California consumers"

**Third-Party Sharing:**
- Shares with service providers, affiliates, business partners
- "It is our policy to require such third parties to process your Personal Information only on our behalf"
- Healthcare provider disclosures under HIPAA Business Associate Agreements

**PHI Handling:**
- Acts as HIPAA Business Associate, processes PHI "solely on behalf of and at the direction of your healthcare provider"
- PHI receives "administrative, physical, and technical safeguards, including encryption and role-based access controls"

**Consent & Revocation:**
- EU/UK residents: "If we are processing your Personal Information based on your consent, you may withdraw your consent at anytime"
- SMS opt-out via "STOP"

### Tokenization & De-Identification Technology

**Methodology:**
- Patent-pending de-identification technology replaces private patient info with encrypted "tokens"
- Tokens cannot be reverse-engineered to reveal original information
- De-identification engine performs: (1) de-identification of dataset (removal/modification of patient info) and (2) insertion of encrypted patient tokens
- Recommended token generation elements: name, date of birth, and gender

**HIPAA Compliance:**
- Software generates tokens "adequately de-identified (pursuant to HIPAA) as previously certified by a third-party expert statistician"
- Uses Expert Determination method
- Licensee responsible for ensuring data sets are "de-identified in accordance with applicable laws, including HIPAA"

**Data Linking:**
- Enables matching patient records across datasets "without ever sharing PII"
- Facilitates 60+ million healthcare records moving between thousands of organizations

## 5. Ocean Protocol

**Sources:** docs.oceanprotocol.com, oceanprotocol.com

### Data NFT & Datatoken Framework

**Core Architecture:**
- Data NFTs (ERC721) represent copyright/exclusive license for data assets ("base IP")
- Datatokens (ERC20) enable decentralized access to data assets (sub-licenses from base IP)
- Multiple datatoken contracts per data NFT, each with different license terms

**Licensing Model:**
- Owning 1.0 datatoken grants consumption rights to corresponding dataset
- Functions as "a sub-license from the base IP"
- License terms specify durations (1 day, 1 week, etc.) or use "good default" settings
- ERC20 fungible standard chosen because "licenses themselves are fungible"

**Publisher Rights:**
- Publishers control when to grant access; data NFTs can exist without datatoken contracts initially
- Publishers create datatoken contracts with specific pricing schemas when ready
- Pricing models: "fixed rate schema" (paid) and "dispenser schema" (free distribution)

**Revenue Model:**
- Revenue goes directly to data NFT owners through datatoken sales
- No platform-imposed revenue split documented in public materials
- Specific fee structures not publicly detailed

**Compute-to-Data (Privacy):**
- "Algorithms are securely transported to the data sources, enabling computation to be performed locally, without the need to expose sensitive data"
- Enables "collaborative data analysis while preserving data privacy"
- Data never leaves the owner's infrastructure

**Data Ownership:**
- "Data owners want to retain control over their valuable assets"
- Ownership tracked on-chain via NFT ownership
- Transferable: NFT can be sold/transferred, transferring base IP rights

### Privacy/Terms

- Privacy policy and terms of use pages returned 404 errors or contained only CSS
- Legal terms appear to be embedded in smart contract interactions rather than traditional ToS documents

## 9. HIPAA De-Identification Standards

**Sources:** HHS.gov, AccountableHQ, HIPAA Journal

### Two Methods

#### Safe Harbor Method
Removes 18 specific identifiers:
1. Names
2. Geographic subdivisions smaller than state (ZIP codes: first 3 digits OK if area >20,000 people)
3. All date elements except year (ages 89+: use "90 or older")
4. Telephone numbers
5. Fax numbers
6. Email addresses
7. Social Security numbers
8. Medical record numbers
9. Health plan beneficiary numbers
10. Account numbers
11. Certificate/license numbers
12. Vehicle identifiers and license plates
13. Device identifiers and serial numbers
14. Web URLs
15. IP addresses
16. Biometric identifiers (fingerprints, voice prints)
17. Full-face photographs and comparable images
18. Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code

**Requirements:**
- Remove all 18 categories
- Maintain "no actual knowledge" remaining data could identify individuals
- Scan free-text fields for residual identifiers
- Aggregate small cell counts to prevent singling out
- Document removal workflow

**Best for:** High-level statistics, quality metrics, broad distribution requiring simple rules

#### Expert Determination Method

**8-Step Workflow:**
1. Define scope and use (purposes, users, sharing channels, duration)
2. Threat modeling (adversaries, auxiliary data)
3. Select metrics (k-anonymity, l-diversity, t-closeness, uniqueness rates)
4. Apply anonymization (suppression, generalization, aggregation, perturbation, noise, swapping, tokenization)
5. Evaluate residual risk
6. Strengthen controls (DUAs, organizational, technical safeguards)
7. Decision and report (written determination if risk "very small")
8. Monitor and re-review

**Expert Qualifications:**
- Advanced training in statistics, biostatistics, computer science, or epidemiology
- Experience with health datasets and common quasi-identifiers
- Ability to justify "very small" risk thresholds
- Professional independence

**Best for:** Granular dates, partial geography, longitudinal linkages, rare-event analysis, real-world evidence

### Best Practices
- Data minimization: retain only necessary information
- Combine technical protections with policy controls
- Plan lifecycle governance: version releases, monitor drift, periodic re-assessments
- Ensure expert independence and reproducible methodology
- Retain documentation aligned with HIPAA record-keeping

### 2026 HIPAA Updates (Effective Feb 16, 2026)
- Stricter privacy protections for reproductive and behavioral health data
- New attestation requirements for PHI disclosures
- Mandatory MFA and encryption for ePHI
- Faster breach reporting from business associates
- Updated Notices of Privacy Practices required

## 10. Health Data Consent Frameworks

### Standard Health Consent (SHC) Platform Model

**Source:** Nature Digital Medicine (2025) - PMC12657888

**Architecture:**
- Centralized consent management infrastructure
- Three components: SHC Connect (embedded in apps), SHC Management App (user dashboard), SHC Service (backend)

**Consent Model: Opt-In/Consent-Based**
- Three models analyzed: (1) permissive without control, (2) opt-out, (3) opt-in/consent-based
- Recommends opt-in: "opt-out mechanisms assume participation by default" and fail to reflect user autonomy

**Granular Consent:**
- Four data categories: body health measures, lifestyle/well-being, personal environment, health insights
- Primary use (healthcare providers) vs. secondary use (pseudonymized for research)
- Users can "allow or restrict pseudonymised data sharing for research and product development" independently

**Dynamic Consent:**
- Central dashboard showing all connected apps and settings
- Individual or bulk update capabilities
- Detailed consent history logs
- "Each given consent can be changed and withdrawn at any time"

**Consent Revocation:**
- Health apps must "act immediately upon withdrawal notifications"
- Contractual framework ensures "prompt honouring of revoked consents"

**Privacy Architecture:**
- User identity and consent data remain disconnected
- Unique user pseudonym per user
- External identity providers authenticated "without storing personal identifiers"
- Manages consent metadata only, not actual health data

**Legal Compliance (GDPR):**
- Informed: details on data controllers, processing purposes, data types
- Specific: purpose-bound consent tied to defined categories
- Revocable: continuous withdrawal capability

**Access Board Model:**
- For secondary use where "exact purposes or recipients may not yet be known"
- Access boards review data requests and "allow processing only after verified application for approved purpose"

### Open Source Tools

**Consent2Share (SAMHSA):**
- Open source consent management and data segmentation
- Integrates with existing EHR and HIE systems
- Enables patients to have "more meaningful choice when sharing their health information"

**gICS (Generic Informed Consent Service):**
- Free, open-source software for digital informed consents
- Covers various research scenarios
- International research community focused

**FHIR-Based Consent Platform:**
- Simplifies consent management with interoperable APIs
- Covers privacy, medical treatment, research, and advance care directives

### All of Us Research Program

- 650,000+ participants, many from historically underrepresented groups
- Employs "blanket" consent: participants agree for data to be used in any future appropriate research
- Community organization partnerships for recruitment
- Broad consent valid for up to 5 years, revocable at any time

## 11. Blockchain Data Marketplace Legal Standards

### Regulatory Landscape (2025-2026)

- By 2026, nearly half of U.S. population covered by state privacy laws
- Eight states rolled out new privacy legislation in 2025
- Requirements include data minimization, protection assessments, compliance documentation

### Key Legal Tension: Immutability vs. Right to Erasure

- Blockchain immutability conflicts with "right to be forgotten" (GDPR) and data deletion requirements
- Solution: Off-chain storage where only encrypted references remain on blockchain; actual data deleted from compliant cloud systems

### Standards & Interoperability

- HL7 FHIR and OpenEHR for clinical data format interoperability
- Hyperledger Fabric for permissioned blockchain networks
- IPFS for distributed storage
- Smart contracts for automated regulatory compliance

### Consent Management via Smart Contracts

- Smart contracts can encode consent rules and automate enforcement
- Incentivize health research through reward infrastructure
- Proof mechanisms (Proof-of-Competence, zero-knowledge proofs) for compliance verification

### HIPRA Legislation (Proposed)

If enacted, would require entities gaining PHI through patient right of access to:
- Inform individuals their data is no longer protected by HIPAA
- Explain how information may be redisclosed
- Require consumer consent before selling data to third parties

## 12. Key Takeaways for XSpan Legal Drafting

### Data Ownership Models (Spectrum)

| Company | User Owns Data? | Revenue Share? | Consent Model |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| 23andMe | No (licensed to company) | No | Opt-in research consent |
| Nebula Genomics | Partial (retains uploaded) | No | Affirmative consent per project |
| Truveta | No (health system contributes) | Health systems are equity owners | HIPAA de-identification (no patient consent needed) |
| Datavant | No (processor role) | No | HIPAA BAA framework |
| Ocean Protocol | Yes (NFT-based ownership) | Yes (direct sales to buyers) | Publisher-controlled |
| HealthBlocks | Yes (stated in terms) | Rewards only (not revenue share) | Explicit consent per partner |
| Hu-manity.co | Mission says yes, terms say no | No | Standard collection consent |
| Solve.Care | Stated in marketing | Unknown | Smart contract-based |

### Critical Legal Provisions XSpan Should Include

1. **Data Ownership Clause:** Explicitly state whether users own their health data and what rights they grant to XSpan (license scope, exclusivity, transferability, revocability).

2. **De-Identification Standard:** Specify which HIPAA method (Safe Harbor or Expert Determination) will be used, and document the process.

3. **Consent Architecture:**
   - Granular consent (per data type, per use case, per partner)
   - Dynamic consent (modifiable at any time)
   - Separate consent for primary use vs. secondary/research use
   - Separate consent for individual-level vs. aggregate data sharing

4. **Consent Revocation:**
   - Clear timeline for revocation to take effect (23andMe uses 30 days)
   - Explicit statement about data already shared/published (cannot be retrieved)
   - No impact on core service access upon revocation

5. **Revenue Sharing Terms:**
   - Explicit percentage or formula (most competitors do NOT share revenue)
   - Payment mechanism and frequency
   - Transparency on who purchases data and for what purpose

6. **Third-Party Sharing Restrictions:**
   - Enumerate permitted categories (research, pharma, device companies, etc.)
   - Prohibit sharing with insurance companies and employers (industry standard)
   - Prohibit re-identification attempts by data recipients
   - Require data use agreements with all recipients

7. **Blockchain-Specific Provisions:**
   - On-chain vs. off-chain data storage explanation
   - Right to erasure implementation (off-chain deletion with on-chain reference invalidation)
   - Smart contract audit and governance terms
   - Token/NFT ownership rights and transferability

8. **HIPAA Compliance:**
   - Business Associate Agreement terms
   - De-identification certification (Expert Determination recommended for data marketplace use)
   - Breach notification procedures (updated for 2026 requirements)
   - Attestation requirements for PHI disclosures (new 2026 requirement)

9. **Research Consent (if applicable):**
   - IRB-approved consent documents (23andMe model)
   - Separate main research consent and individual data sharing consent
   - Clear statement that commercial products from research do NOT generate user compensation (or explicitly state if they do)

10. **Immutability Disclaimer:**
    - "Knowledge is irrevocable" concept (23andMe model)
    - Once data is analyzed/published, results cannot be reversed
    - Blockchain transaction records are permanent

### Recommended Legal Document Set for XSpan

1. **Terms of Service** - Platform usage, account terms, general legal framework
2. **Privacy Policy** - Data collection, use, sharing, security, user rights
3. **Data Use Agreement (Template)** - For data recipients/researchers
4. **Main Consent Form** - General data sharing consent with granular options
5. **Research Consent Form** - IRB-approvable consent for secondary research use
6. **Individual Data Sharing Consent** - Consent for individual-level (not just aggregate) data sharing
7. **Publisher/Seller Agreement** - Terms for users who sell/license their data on the marketplace
8. **Data NFT License Terms** - Default and customizable license terms for tokenized data assets
9. **Business Associate Agreement** - HIPAA BAA for covered entity relationships
10. **Community Standards** - Acceptable use, re-identification prohibition, data handling requirements
